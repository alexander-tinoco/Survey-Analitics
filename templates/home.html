Three questions, answered in order

  1. How did people answer?

    Distributions, participation, and the questions people skipped — because a result computed from half the room should say so.

  2. What goes with what?

    Every pair of questions cross-tabulated, ranked by how strong the relationship is rather than by how large the sample was.

  3. Who resembles whom?

    Respondent profiles, and which questions divide the room into camps rather than merely leaving it undecided.

The harder half

On 30 respondents of random answers, this reports nothing at all.

A chi-square returns a plausible number for any two columns, and most tools print it. A finding is withheld here unless it clears its own statistical assumptions, survives correction for the number of comparisons made, and covers enough respondents to describe someone.

Nothing found is a result, and it is written down as one.
